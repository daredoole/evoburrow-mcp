# Speaker and room engineering

## Placement before correction

- Establish the listening position and speaker geometry before calibration. Use the exact Dolby diagram for the selected layout; broad angular windows are screening aids.
- Keep listener-level tweeters near seated ear height when practical. Aim the center toward the listening area and avoid burying it in a reflective enclosure.
- Evaluate toe-in from horizontal directivity and off-axis response, not a universal angle. Use matched left/right geometry where the room permits.
- Check speaker-boundary interference by moving speakers or seats and remeasuring; equalization cannot reliably fill a deep cancellation caused by geometry.

## Crossover and output capability

- A published low-frequency extension is not automatically a safe crossover. Prefer anechoic/on-axis F3 plus distortion, compression, listening distance, and target SPL.
- Starting near 1.5-2× speaker F3 often improves headroom, but verify crossover-region summation and localization in the actual room.
- Positive trim and filter boost consume headroom. Correct broad placement/setup problems before demanding large electrical boosts.
- Multiple subwoofers primarily improve spatial consistency when placed and aligned well; added quantity alone does not guarantee smoother bass.

## Measurement practice

- Preserve raw measurements. Record microphone calibration, orientation, sample rate, sweep level, seat, environmental noise, and processing state.
- Diagnose consistency before equalization: clipping, low signal, timing shifts, polarity changes, and seat outliers undermine later conclusions.
- Use psychoacoustically appropriate smoothing for interpretation while retaining unsmoothed data for modal and narrow-band diagnosis.
- Validate changes with before/after measurements and level-matched listening. A preference result is not a universal engineering rule.

Sources: Dolby Atmos Home Theater Installation Guidelines; REW Help; local A1 artifacts.
